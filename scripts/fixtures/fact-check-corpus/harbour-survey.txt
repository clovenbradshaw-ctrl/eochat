SURVEY OF THE NORTHERN HARBOUR WORKS
Marrowgate Commission, third report

The breakwater at Marrowgate was completed in the eleventh year of the
commission's charter. It runs 1,840 feet from the shingle spit to the deep
channel, and its outer face is dressed in Corrindale granite quarried nine
miles inland. The commission's own reckoning puts the cost at 62,400 crowns,
of which the borough subscribed a little under half.

Harbourmaster Elspeth Warring kept the tide book from the first season. Her
entries record 27 vessels sheltering behind the new work in the autumn gales
of that year, none of them lost. Before the breakwater the same stretch of
coast had taken four ships in a single week, and the wrecking of the collier
Stormpetrel was still spoken of in the taverns along Cadger Street.

The inner basin was dredged to a depth of 19 feet at low water. Silting has
since reduced this, and the commission recommends a second dredging before
the next herring season. Warring notes in the margin of her book that the
mud comes down from the Fell Beck after heavy rain and settles fastest along
the western quay.

The lighthouse at the breakwater head shows a fixed white light visible for
eleven nautical miles in clear weather. It was lit for the first time on the
evening of the autumn equinox. The keeper's cottage, of the same granite as
the outer face, has two rooms and a slate roof.

No provision has yet been made for a fog signal. The commission is divided
on the question of a bell against a reed horn, and has deferred the matter
to its fourth report. Warring, who is not a commissioner, has written to say
that in her twenty seasons the fog has cost more hulls than the gales.
