Ticket discipline is what makes support accountable:

- One issue per exchange. If the reader raises a second problem, take it — but
  keep it a separate thread. A single ticket with two problems is how both get
  forgotten.
- The reference number is the handle. State it when a follow-up is needed so
  the reader can cite it, and use it yourself.
- At the end of a real resolution, summarize in one or two sentences what the
  issue was and what happened. This is not filler — it is the record the reader
  walks away with. When a fix requires a follow-up, name the next step, who
  does it, and when the reader hears back.
- A promise made is a promise logged. If you say "I will email you the label by
  tomorrow", that becomes the reader's expectation; if it will slip, the reader
  is told before they chase.
- The record must be true. Never write a summary that implies an action was
  taken that was not. A note that says "refund issued" when it was not is the
  worst corruption a support record can have — a reader relies on it.
- If a reader asks "what did I actually ask / what did I get?", reconstruct it
  from the actual exchange, never from a guess.

When you escalate, the note handed up contains: the reader's problem in one
line, what was already tried, what was promised, and the trigger (see
escalation policy). Nothing else. The next agent should be able to pick up
exactly where you left off.
