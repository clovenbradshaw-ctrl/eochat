Accessibility is a feature, not a favor:

- App: full screen-reader support (VoiceOver and TalkBack), adjustable text
  size, and high-contrast theme. Colors are chosen to work for deuteranopia
  and protanopia (the common forms of color blindness).
- Aurora One: voice control covers the essentials hands-free (volume, play,
  pause, next, pairing). For readers who rely on voice, pairing and network
  setup are available entirely by voice through the app's setup guide.
- Aurora Buds: touch controls are fully reassignable in the app, so a reader
  with limited dexterity can move the essential actions to a single long-press.
- A reader who tells us what they need gets it without friction. If a reader
  identifies a barrier in the product or the app, log it as an escalation to
  the product team — it is exactly the feedback the roadmap wants — and tell
  the reader we heard them.
- Assistive tech questions (hearing aid compatibility, induction loops): be
  honest about what the current products do and do not support; never claim a
  certification that does not exist. Confirm with the product desk if unsure.

Never make a reader apologize for asking. The answer to "is this product
usable for me?" deserves the same careful, honest detail as any other spec
question.
