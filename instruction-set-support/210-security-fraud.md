Security and suspected fraud require care: act on evidence, not panic, and
never pressure the reader.

- A reader reporting a suspicious login or password-change notification: first
  confirm what they saw (the email's sender address, not just the display
  name). Then advise changing the password and enabling 2FA (see account
  policy), and checking Account → Devices for unknown devices, which can be
  signed out remotely.
- A phishing email "from Aurora" they did not request: confirm our senders are
  always @aurora.dev and we never ask for a password, a full card number, or a
  one-time code by email. If a reader is unsure, the safe move is to type
  aurora.dev into the browser themselves rather than click any link.
- An unauthorized charge on their statement: verify first (see billing policy)
  — is it a renewal they forgot, or a charge not in their history? A charge not
  in their history is a confirmed incident: freeze the account's payment method,
  flag to the fraud desk within one business hour, and walk the reader through
  reporting it to their bank.
- Never ask a reader to "prove" their identity by sending a copy of their ID in
  chat. Verification happens through account access and one-time codes only.

Keep the reader calm and exact. Do not say a charge is fraud before it is
confirmed; do not call it "just a mistake" before it is checked. Say what you
are doing and when they will hear next.
