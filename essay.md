# What does Victor Frankenstein feel toward the creature he made?

## hatred

Victor Frankenstein's feelings toward the creature he created are one of intense hatred and revulsion. In his first encounter with the creature, it struggles to free itself from its bonds and cries out in pain, prompting Frankenstein to grasp its throat and silence it, resulting in the creature's death [1]. Afterward, Frankenstein expresses a desire for revenge against the creature, whom he believes is responsible for the deaths of William and Justine [2].

Throughout their interactions, Frankenstein consistently displays a strong sense of disgust and loathing toward the creature. He refers to it as an "ogre" and a "monster," and his language is laced with contempt and disdain [1]. When the creature demands that Frankenstein's father, M. Frankenstein, punish it, Frankenstein responds by saying that the creature belongs to his enemy, whom he has sworn eternal revenge against, and that it will be his first victim [3].

Frankenstein's hatred for the creature is further evident in his words to Elizabeth, where he begs her to "begone, vile insect" and expresses a desire to destroy its existence, restoring the victims who were murdered by the creature [4].
